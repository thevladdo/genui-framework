# Northwind Claims Procedure

A claim is a request for compensation for a parcel that was lost, damaged or
delivered late. Service credits for missed transit commitments are a different
thing entirely and are handled through the contract, not here.

## Who may claim

Only the contracting party may claim, which is the sender in almost every case.
A recipient who was not the contracting party is redirected to the sender, and
the redirection is the most frequent source of complaint about this process.

## Time limits

A claim for damage is raised within 7 days of delivery. A claim for loss is
raised no earlier than 21 days after the tender date, because a parcel that has
not moved for three weeks is treated as lost rather than delayed.

The outer limit for any claim is 6 months from the tender date. Nothing is
accepted after that, regardless of the circumstances.

## Evidence

Damage claims require photographs of the packaging as well as the contents.
Photographs of the contents alone cannot show whether the packaging was
adequate, and that is the question the assessment turns on.

Loss claims require the tender record and the commercial value. A retail
invoice is accepted; an internal transfer price is not.

## Assessment

Liability is limited by weight unless a declared value was purchased at tender.
Most customers discover the weight limit at their first significant claim.

Declared value is priced per hundred euro of cover and must be purchased before
the parcel is collected. It cannot be added retrospectively.

## Excluded goods

Cash, documents that cannot be reissued, and unpackaged fragile items are
excluded. The exclusion list is short on purpose and is printed on the tender
manifest.

Perishables are excluded once they leave the temperature controlled network,
which happens whenever a recipient refuses delivery.

## Outcome

A decision is issued within 30 days of a complete claim. Incomplete claims are
not refused: they are held, and the clock does not start until the missing
evidence arrives.

A rejected claim can be escalated once, to a reviewer who did not take the
first decision.
